## Introduction

As a 19 year old Interactive Media student with minors in Film and New Media and Design, I think digital literacy is something I use every day without always thinking of it as a skill. I already know how to use tools like Google Drive, Canva, Adobe programs, p5.js, GitHub, and social media, but I am realizing that knowing how to use a tool is not the same as understanding how it affects the way I create and communicate. For content creation, I am comfortable using digital tools to make visual and interactive work. In my classes, I have been learning p5.js and basic HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, which has made me think differently about what it means to create something digitally. Instead of only making an image or video, I can make something that responds to a person. I also use editing and design software for film and visual projects. Social media has taught me how to communicate quickly and notice how people react to different kinds of content. At the same time, I sometimes use these platforms automatically without thinking about how algorithms influence what I see.


## Research and Data Management
Research and data management are areas where I still have a lot to learn. I know how to organize files in Google Drive and use spreadsheets, but I never really thought much about how important the way we organize data actually is. In the reading <a href="https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.2017.1375989">Data Organization in Spreadsheets</a>, Broman and Woo explain how small things like inconsistent naming, leaving cells empty, or putting too much information in one cell can make data harder to work with and lead to errors. I think I used to see spreadsheets as just a place to store information, but this reading made me realize that organizing data properly is just as important as analyzing it. It also made me think about how easily small mistakes can affect the results, especially when working with large amounts of information. I still want to improve my skills in data analysis and visualization, but I think understanding how to organize data correctly is an important starting point.


## Learning From Different Perspectives

I think having students from different majors in the same class is interesting because everyone has their own way of thinking and approaching things. For example, someone studying Computer Science might focus more on coding and how something works, while someone studying Film and New Media might care more about the visuals and how a story is told. I think bringing these different perspectives together can help us come up with ideas we might not have thought of on our own. It also helps me see that there isn't always one way to solve a problem, and I can learn a lot from how other people approach the same task differently. I think this is especially important for Interactive Media because it combines both the creative and technical sides of technology. Sometimes I find myself focusing more on how something looks or how someone will interact with it, rather than thinking about everything happening behind it. Being around people who approach things differently can help me notice things I might have overlooked. It's nice to know that two people can be given the exact same assignment but come up with different ideas depending on their interests and experiences and this is something that i've come to realize through my progression in this course. I think being in a class with people from different backgrounds helps me become more open-minded and consider ideas or approaches that I wouldn't normally think of myself.

## Working With a Science Fiction Corpus

One of the things we've done in Digital Humanities that made me think differently about analyzing information was working with a **corpus of science fiction texts**. Instead of reading each text individually, we used digital tools like <a href="https://posit.cloud/">Posit Cloud</a> and <a href="https://www.r-project.org/">R</a> to look at patterns in words across different stories and time periods. We explored things like word frequency and KWIC, which helped us see how certain words were being used in different contexts. I think what stood out to me was that just because a word appears a lot doesn't necessarily mean it has the same meaning every time. We still need to look at the context and think about what the results are actually telling us. This made me realize that digital tools can help us find patterns, but they can't do all the interpreting for us. Something I remember from this assignment was how my partner and I initially used ChatGPT to help us come up with words to analyze. We chose words like "dystopia," thinking they would appear frequently in science fiction stories, but when we looked at the results, the word didn't appear at all. We eventually changed our word choices because they weren't giving us the results we expected. I think this was a good example of how AI can give us a starting point


Another reading that connected to what we did in class was <a href="https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/9522.001.0001">The Measured Words: How Computers Analyze Text</a>by Rockwell and Sinclair. The authors explain how computers analyze texts by looking at patterns, counting words, and organizing information, but that doesn't mean they actually understand what they're reading. I think this connects really well to our science fiction corpus assignment because we were using tools to find words that appeared frequently and looking at how they were used in different contexts. One thing that stood out to me from the reading was how word frequency doesn't always tell us how important something is in a text. For example, a word might appear many times, but that doesn't necessarily mean it has the same meaning throughout the story. This made me realize that even though computers can make analyzing large amounts of text much easier, we still have to think about what the results actually mean.


## How My Understanding of Digital Literacy Has Changed
Before taking Digital Humanities, I thought being digitally literate mostly meant knowing how to use different programs and being comfortable with technology. Since I already use digital tools for my Interactive Media and Film projects, I didn't really think there was much more to it. However, throughout this course, I've started to realize that digital literacy is also about understanding how technology works, how information is collected and organized, and how it can influence what we see online. Moving forward, I want to become more aware of these things, especially when using AI. I already use AI when I need help understanding something, brainstorming ideas, or figuring out problems with my code, but I don't want to rely on it for everything. I think it's important to question the information it gives me rather than automatically assuming it's correct. I also want to continue improving my coding and data analysis skills and explore how I can combine them with my interests in film and design. I think there's still a lot I don't know about technology, and that's something I want to keep learning about.


## Comparing ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude
When comparing the reflections written by **ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude**, I noticed that some of the information they included wasn't actually true about me. For example, one of the responses mentioned that I had been editing videos since I was fourteen, while another talked about using programs like Tableau and Datawrapper, which I haven't used. I thought it was strange how confidently the AI described these experiences even though they weren't mine. Some of the writing also sounded much more formal than how I would normally express myself. I think this assignment helped me realize that even when AI produces something that sounds convincing, it doesn't mean everything is accurate. I still find AI useful, especially when I'm struggling with coding or need ideas, but I think it's important to go through what it generates and make sure it actually reflects my own thoughts and experiences.


## Reflection and Conclusion
Overall, I think my digital literacy is strongest when I am experimenting and learning by buildings things, but I still have a lot to improve when it comes to data management, analysis, and understanding what happens behind the tools I use. This course is making me think of the ways how digital literacy allows me to question technology, understand information, work with people who think differently, and make decisions about how digital tools should be used. I want to explore more ways of combining code, design, film, data, and AI instead of keeping them separate. I think that is where I can develop my own way of thinking as an Interactive Media student.
